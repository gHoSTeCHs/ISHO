How self-help works

It begins with a passbook, not a handout.

A woman joins a primary group of her neighbours. The group guarantees one another, receives a first small loan, and keeps its own books — every naira in and out recorded by hand. Repayments fund the next member. Training comes alongside the money.

Over a cycle, the stall grows: more stock, a second table, a hired hand. The loan is repaid not out of charity but out of trade — and the ledger proves it.

"Finding alternatives for the poor." — the founding theme, 1984, and still the test we hold every programme to.

How we got here

Forty years, four turning points.

1984

An idea on paper

ISHO begins as an academic project at the Centre for Applied Religion and Education, Ibadan, under the theme "Finding Alternatives for the Poor," coordinated by Godson C. Echefu.

1989

Into the field

The work begins informally as the Mbaitoli-Ikeduru Self Help Organization (MISHO), serving the two local government areas it was named for.

1990

Imo Self Help Organization is born

Registered with the Imo State Ministry of Trade & Industries, Cooperative Division — and renamed for the whole state as its scope widened.

1995

On the national register

Incorporated with the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) — the legal backing that opened doors to international partners and a far larger reach.
